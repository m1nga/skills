# Troubleshooting & techniques

## Clumping / static (dry-looking powder, clumps, clinging, spraying)
**Cause**: static. Light-roast beans are dense and low-moisture; add dry air and the
grounds come out charged — clumping, spraying everywhere, sticking inside the grinder.
Clumps also wreck distribution → channeling → extraction swings shot to shot.

**Fix: RDT (Ross Droplet Technique)**
- **Before** grinding, dip a toothpick or spoon tip in **one drop of water** and stir the
  beans (or one ultra-light mist)
- One drop is enough — the goal is discharging static, not wetting beans
- Effect: clumping and spraying essentially gone, retention way down, distribution
  evener, extraction steadier
- Light roast + dry season: treat it as standard practice

## WDT (Weiss Distribution Technique)
After grinding into the basket, use a fine needle (a purpose-made WDT tool, or a few pins)
to gently break up clumps and level the bed, then tamp. Removes grinder-induced clumps
and density unevenness — the single most effective channeling prevention.

## Channeling
**Symptom**: water shortcuts through weak spots/cracks in the puck → locally
over-extracted + globally under-extracted → the shot sprays sideways (squirts), flow
jumps around, taste is sour AND astringent at once.
**Causes**: uneven distribution, clumps, tilted tamp, overfilled basket touching the
shower screen, gaps at the basket edge.
**Fixes**:
- RDT + WDT
- Tamp level, consistent pressure
- Don't overfill the basket (touching the shower screen = spraying) → drop 1g if needed
- If available, watch a shot with a naked (bottomless) portafilter — squirts are
  channeling, visible directly

## Extraction taste diagnosis (full version)
| | Under-extracted | Over-extracted |
|--|--|--|
| Taste | sour, sharp, astringent, salty, hollow, not sweet, dry finish | bitter, burnt, woody, acrid, ashy, dry-bitter aftertaste |
| Usual causes | too coarse, too fast, too short, water too cool, dense light roast not fully extracted | too fine, too slow, too long, water too hot |
| Rescue | grind finer, longer ratio/time, temp up | grind coarser, shorter ratio/time, temp down |

**Key reminder**: both taste "unpleasant" on first sip and are easily confused.
**Mainly sour → under-extracted; mainly bitter → over-extracted.** A light roast's
under-extracted sharpness is routinely mistaken for over-extraction — check the two
objective numbers (**time + grind setting**) before ruling.

## Fresh-bean degassing (rested <7 days)
Very fresh beans hold a lot of CO₂: espresso channels easily, pour-over blooms erupt.
- Espresso: rest 7–10 days before dialing in seriously; if needed now, grind half a step
  coarser and expect a slightly reduced extraction
- Pour-over: more bloom water, longer bloom (35–45s)

## Shot / crema appearance
- Light roasts: thin, pale crema is NORMAL — don't call the shot failed on crema alone
- Blonde from the start, fast flow → under-extraction or channeling
- Extremely slow, near-black, almost no crema → over-extraction or ground too fine
  (choked)
