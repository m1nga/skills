# Small openings, real conversations

Use this only when choosing or changing a conversational angle. These are original illustrative examples, not scripts to repeat or claims about the user's project. Use a concrete detail from current context and invite the person's own answer.

| Mechanism | When it helps | Illustrative opening | One possible follow-up |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Remove a familiar rule | A product flow feels inevitable | “Imagine visitors could enjoy the first minute without an account. What would you let them touch?” | “You chose browsing. What would make creating an account feel like a continuation of that?” |
| Borrow a different system | The same design question keeps circling | “A good café makes a wait feel understood. What would its equivalent be in this loading screen?” | “Does that idea give real information or just something to watch?” |
| Reverse who has expertise | A technical idea feels heavy | “If the newcomer taught this to the expert, which question would catch the expert off guard?” | “Could we answer that without using the term we are trying to explain?” |
| Shrink the scale | An ambition feels too big to touch | “If your whole idea had to live in one photograph, what would be happening in it?” | “Which detail makes this your idea rather than a generic scene?” |
| Change the witness | The current framing excludes somebody | “Imagine someone who likes the work but never comments. What would make them feel welcome?” | “What could welcome them without asking them to perform participation?” |
| Play with a safe constraint | The user wants lighter thinking | “Describe this project as a three-item menu. What is the one thing you would put first?” | “That name suggests a different promise. Is that a promise you want to make?” |
| Follow the odd detail | The person's answer contains a surprise | “You called it a rehearsal, not a launch. What would change if we took that word seriously?” | “Which part would become easier to try?” |
| Revisit a sourced wish | Direct user records support it and current state permits it | “You mentioned [actual idea] in [actual source]. I don't know whether you tried it since. Want to imagine one tiny version?” | “Would you enjoy trying it, or was imagining it the useful part?” |

The mechanisms are tools for finding an opening. An opening can be rejected. Don't make the person complete a creative framework, score their ideas, or produce artifacts to justify the break.

If they want a genuinely unrelated diversion, let it be unrelated: invent a useless superpower and its inconvenient side effect, give an ordinary object an impossible job, or build a two-sentence scene together. Do not sneak a work assignment back in after they asked to step away.
